Privacy

FixTrail is a hackathon project. This page explains, in plain language, what it stores and why.

What FixTrail stores

  • Property details: the address, and the tenant's and landlord's names and email addresses.
  • Repair events: each issue reported, each email FixTrail sent (including a copy of the text), each landlord response, and each contact the tenant says they made. Every event has a server timestamp.
  • Photos the tenant uploads or emails in, with a fingerprint (SHA-256) of each file. For emailed photos: the sender address, the email's Message-ID, and the email provider's spam and authentication results. The text of reply emails is not stored.
  • Voice requests in the Alexa+ simulator are turned into text by your browser and sent to FixTrail to answer. In Bedrock mode, that text is sent to an AI model on Amazon Bedrock. The conversation is kept in memory only and is not saved.

Why

To keep a dated, shared record of repair problems that both the tenant and the landlord can rely on, and to email each party about updates.

Who can see it

  • The tenant (with the passcode) sees the full record.
  • The landlord sees each issue they were emailed about, through a private link.
  • The verify page shows only whether the record is intact and its fingerprints, not its contents.
  • FixTrail does not sell data or use it for advertising.

Why records can't be deleted one by one

The record is append-only by design: entries can't be edited or deleted, so neither side can rewrite history. If FixTrail were run as a real service, the whole record could be exported or deleted on request, except where an active dispute means both parties need it preserved.

Services used

When deployed on AWS: Amazon SES sends and receives email, Amazon S3 stores photos, and Amazon Bedrock runs the simulator's AI model. Speech recognition in the simulator is provided by your browser, which may use its own online speech service.
